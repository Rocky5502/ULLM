\paragraph{Stage-5 execution record and contract adjudication (2026-09-03).}
The full neutral deterministic stage completed all 2,000 planned calls (400 benchmark items across five gateway routes) before the hard audit stopped the pipeline. The pre-adjudication raw directory was sealed successfully in \texttt{frozen-det-neutral-v1.checksums.predecision.json} (six files; 6,495,443 bytes). Request/schema validity was otherwise complete for the five model files. The strict response-contract audit identified a distinct phenomenon: the explicit discrete \texttt{label} disagreed with the model's own highest reported probability on 10/400 Claude responses (2.5\%) and 8/400 Qwen responses (2.0\%). GPT, DeepSeek, and Gemini had zero such disagreements in Stage~5. Gemini recorded 31 successful responses that required more than one HTTP attempt; these remained successful provider records and are retained as operational provenance rather than discarded.

Inspection of all 18 disagreement rows showed that these were not parser failures, missing fields, probability-sum failures, or truncations. Each contained a valid label, three probabilities, and a short reason. The disagreements were substantive rather than exclusively rounding ties. For Claude the ten rows occurred in groups A/C/D as 2/5/3; for Qwen they occurred in groups A/B as 7/1. Across the two affected routes, the disagreements therefore concentrated most heavily in interrupted accomplishments (group A), with additional cases in ambiguous accomplishments and activities. This pattern is retained as an exploratory decision--distribution faithfulness diagnostic rather than converted post hoc into a preregistered hypothesis.

\paragraph{Adjudication A1.}
Selective re-querying of only the 18 inconsistent rows would condition data collection on an observed model behavior and could replace difficult items. Likewise, overwriting a provider-emitted label with its probability argmax would alter the response after observation. We therefore preserve every schema-valid response exactly as returned. The explicit \texttt{label} remains the primary discrete decision for accuracy, TBR/$\Delta$AA, paired correctness, repeated-sampling labels, prompt/order flips, error targets, and verifier decisions. The probability vector remains the continuous uncertainty report for SUR, TOR, ADG, Brier/NLL, classwise calibration, entropy/1-max-probability, JSD, and selective-risk scoring. Label--probability disagreement is separately quantified and never silently repaired. The original strict audit remains available; continuation requires an explicit adjudication flag that converts only this specific contract inconsistency to a recorded warning while all request, parsing, probability, manifest, K-completeness, completion-budget, and model-control failures remain fatal.

Because ``top-label ECE'' refers to the class with maximum reported probability, its calibration computation is tied to the probability argmax rather than to a separately verbalized label when the two interfaces disagree. Discrete accuracy-style metrics continue to use the explicit label. This distinction is fixed before the K=5 sampling, robustness, and verifier stages and is independent of which representation happens to score better against gold labels.
